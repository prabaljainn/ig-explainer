# _example script

## Narration
How did someone measure the Earth 2,200 years ago with a stick?
At noon in Syene, the sun stood straight overhead. No shadow.
The same day in Alexandria, a vertical stick cast a short shadow.
The shadow gave the sun's angle from vertical: about 7.2 degrees.
Sun rays are parallel, so that angle also sits at Earth's center.
7.2 degrees is one fiftieth of a full circle.
The two cities were about 800 kilometers apart.
Fifty of those arcs make the whole circumference.
That gives 40,000 kilometers. The true value is 40,075.
One shadow, one angle, and geometry did the rest.

## Scenes
- 0: hook headline above the Earth; the circle draws itself in white over the first second, then the first sun ray travels in from the left
- 1: the ray meets the circle at Syene; the stick points straight at the sun; label Syene just inside the rim
- 2: second ray meets the circle at Alexandria (24 deg, exaggerated); tilted stick, a short shadow on the ground; label Alexandria inside the rim
- 3: small arc between the stick and the ray at Alexandria; label 7.2 deg; dim note "angle exaggerated for clarity" at the top
- 4: radii to both cities; the same arc appears at the centre; label 7.2 deg under the vertex
- 5: rays, sticks, shadow and the note fade; Alexandria slides along the rim to the true 7.2 deg, the centre arc shrinks with it; centre label reads 1 / 50
- 6: the circle dims; the arc between the cities lights white on the rim; label 800 km outside the rim
- 7: the circle goes faint; fifty white arcs light up around the rim one by one from Syene; counter n / 50
- 8: every mark leaves; the number counts to 40,000 km inside the ring, then "true value 40,075 km" appears under it in grey
- 9: the ring fades, the Earth stays as a ghost; takeaway headline starts after the ghosting is done
